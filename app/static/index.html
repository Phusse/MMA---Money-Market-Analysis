📈 Stock Trading Basics

What are Stocks and Shares?

A stock represents ownership in a company. When you buy shares of a company, you become a partial owner, entitled to a portion of the company's profits (dividends) and assets.

How Stock Exchanges Work

Stock exchanges like the NYSE, NASDAQ, or NGX are marketplaces where buyers and sellers trade shares. Prices are determined by supply and demand - when more people want to buy than sell, prices rise.

Types of Orders

  • Market Order: Buy or sell immediately at the current market price. Best for liquid stocks.
  • Limit Order: Set your desired price. The order executes only when the stock reaches that price.
  • Stop-Loss Order: Automatically sell if the price drops to a specified level, protecting against losses.

Understanding Bid-Ask Spread

The bid price is what buyers are willing to pay, while the ask price is what sellers want. The difference (spread) represents the transaction cost. Tighter spreads mean more liquidity.

💡 Pro Tip

Always use limit orders for less liquid stocks to avoid paying more than expected. Market orders work best for highly-traded stocks like Apple or MTN Nigeria.

💰 How to Start Investing

Opening a Brokerage Account

You need a brokerage account to buy and sell stocks. For US markets, popular options include Robinhood, Fidelity, and Interactive Brokers. For Nigerian stocks, you'll need a CSCS account through a registered stockbroker.

How Much Should You Invest?

  • Only invest money you can afford to lose or won't need for 5+ years
  • Start with an amount you're comfortable with, even if it's small
  • Keep 3-6 months of expenses in an emergency fund first
  • Never invest borrowed money in stocks

Building a Diversified Portfolio

Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Spread investments across different sectors, company sizes, and geographies to reduce risk.

Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)

Instead of investing a lump sum, invest fixed amounts regularly (weekly or monthly). This reduces the impact of market volatility and removes the pressure of timing the market.

💡 Getting Started

Begin with index funds or ETFs like SPY (US) or bank stocks on NGX. They provide instant diversification and are ideal for beginners.

🇳🇬 Nigerian Stock Exchange (NGX)

About the Nigerian Exchange

The Nigerian Exchange Group (NGX) is the leading stock exchange in West Africa with over 150 listed companies across sectors like banking, oil & gas, cement, and consumer goods.

How to Trade on NGX

  1. Open a CSCS Account: Central Securities Clearing System account stores your shares electronically
  2. Choose a Stockbroker: Register with an SEC-licensed stockbroker like Meristem, Stanbic IBTC Securities, or Cardinalstone
  3. Fund Your Account: Transfer money to your broker's account
  4. Place Orders: Instruct your broker to buy/sell shares on your behalf

Trading Hours

NGX operates Monday to Friday, 10:00 AM to 2:30 PM WAT. There's a pre-opening session at 9:30 AM and a closing auction at 2:30 PM.

Top Nigerian Stockbrokers

  • Meristem Stockbrokers
  • Stanbic IBTC Stockbrokers
  • Cardinalstone Partners
  • Chapel Hill Denham
  • Afrinvest Securities

🇳🇬 NGX Tip

Blue-chip Nigerian stocks like Dangote Cement, MTN Nigeria, and tier-1 banks offer relatively stable returns. Many also pay regular dividends.

🛡️ Risk Management

Position Sizing Rules

Never risk more than 1-2% of your total portfolio on a single trade. This way, even a series of losing trades won't devastate your account.

Setting Stop-Loss Orders

  • Fixed Percentage: Set stop-loss at 7-10% below your entry price
  • Technical Levels: Place stops below key support levels
  • Trailing Stops: Move your stop-loss up as the price rises to lock in profits

Portfolio Diversification

Spread your investments across:

  • Different sectors (banking, tech, consumer goods, energy)
  • Different markets (US, Nigerian, emerging markets)
  • Different asset classes (stocks, bonds, cash)

Managing Trading Emotions

Fear and greed are a trader's worst enemies. Create a trading plan and stick to it. Never make impulsive decisions based on emotions.

⚠️ Important Warning

Never average down on a losing position. If a trade goes against you, accept the loss and move on. Cutting losses quickly is key to long-term survival.

📊 Technical Analysis Basics

Candlestick Charts

Each candlestick shows 4 prices: Open, High, Low, Close (OHLC). Green/white candles indicate the price closed higher than it opened, while red/black candles show the opposite.

Support and Resistance

  • Support: Price levels where buying pressure historically prevents further decline
  • Resistance: Price levels where selling pressure historically prevents further rise
  • These levels often become self-fulfilling as traders watch them

Moving Averages

Moving averages smooth out price data to identify trends:

  • 50-day MA: Short-term trend indicator
  • 200-day MA: Long-term trend indicator
  • Golden Cross: Bullish signal when 50-day crosses above 200-day
  • Death Cross: Bearish signal when 50-day crosses below 200-day

Volume Analysis

Volume confirms price movements. Rising prices on high volume is bullish, rising prices on low volume may indicate weakness.

💡 Analysis Tip

Technical analysis works best when combined with fundamental analysis. Look at both the chart patterns AND the company's financials before making decisions.

📖 Trading Glossary

Market Terms

  • Bull Market: A market where prices are rising or expected to rise
  • Bear Market: A market where prices are falling (usually 20%+ decline)
  • Volatility: The degree of price fluctuation in a stock or market
  • Liquidity: How easily a stock can be bought or sold without affecting its price

Valuation Metrics

  • P/E Ratio: Price-to-Earnings ratio. Lower P/E often indicates undervaluation
  • Market Cap: Total value of a company's shares (share price × shares outstanding)
  • Dividend Yield: Annual dividend payment as a percentage of stock price
  • EPS: Earnings Per Share - company profit divided by number of shares

Trading Actions

  • IPO: Initial Public Offering - when a company first sells shares to the public
  • Stock Split: When a company divides existing shares into multiple shares
  • Short Selling: Betting that a stock's price will decline
  • Margin Trading: Borrowing money from your broker to buy stocks

⚠️ Caution

Short selling and margin trading are advanced strategies with significant risk. Only experienced traders should use them.
